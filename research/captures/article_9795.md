H1: Lost Weight With Ozempic or Mounjaro: Why Patients Are Considering Body Contouring in Mexico?
BODY:
Losing a significant amount of weight with medications such as semaglutide or tirzepatide can change far more than the number on the scale. For some people, reaching a lower weight reveals another concern they did not expect: loose abdominal skin, hanging upper arms, sagging thighs, changes in breast shape, or excess skin around the waist and lower body.
That shift is now influencing plastic surgery demand. In 2025, 82% of American Society of Plastic Surgeons member surgeons said they had received consultation requests related to GLP-1 medication use. Tummy tuck, breast surgery, arm lift, thigh lift and body lift were among the procedures most commonly discussed with these patients.
For patients in the United States and Canada in particular, the next question is increasingly not just "Can this loose skin be treated?" but also "Should I have body contouring at home or consider surgery in Mexico?"
Mexico has become relevant because of its proximity to North America, established plastic-surgery market, range of surgical centers and potentially lower treatment costs. However, body contouring after major weight loss can involve extensive surgery, so choosing a destination should never be based on price alone.

Quick Answer
Body contouring after GLP-1-related weight loss can remove excess skin and reshape areas such as the abdomen, arms, thighs, breasts and lower body. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons reports substantial growth in several lift procedures alongside rapidly increasing GLP-1-related consultations.
Patients may consider Mexico because treatment can cost less than in the United States or Canada and because cities such as Tijuana, Mexicali, Monterrey, Mexico City and Cancun have established international plastic-surgery markets.
However, the safest decision is based on the surgeon's specialist credentials, facility authorization, anesthesia team, surgical plan, recovery arrangements and complication-management strategy rather than the lowest advertised package price.

Why GLP-1 Weight Loss Is Changing Plastic Surgery?
GLP-1 and related medications have transformed obesity treatment.
Semaglutide and tirzepatide are examples of medications used for diabetes and, under specific brand names and indications, chronic weight management. In the United States, for example, Wegovy is approved for chronic weight management, while Ozempic is approved for type 2 diabetes. Tirzepatide is marketed as Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes and Zepbound for chronic weight management.
For some patients, these medications can produce substantial weight reduction.
But losing body fat does not necessarily mean the skin contracts at the same rate.
Skin elasticity varies according to factors such as:
Age
Genetics
How much weight was lost
How quickly weight changed
How long the person previously lived at a higher weight
Pregnancy history
Smoking
Sun exposure
Nutrition
The area of the body involved
After substantial weight loss, skin and supporting tissues may no longer conform completely to the person's smaller body size. ASPS identifies this as one of the main reasons people consider surgical body contouring after significant weight loss.
For some patients, this becomes the unexpected second phase of weight loss.
Phase one is losing weight.
Phase two is deciding what to do about the body changes left behind.

What Happens to the Body After Major Weight Loss?
The effects can appear almost anywhere.
Someone may reach a healthy or significantly lower weight but still have:
Abdomen
A fold or apron of loose skin can remain below the waist. In some people it can interfere with clothing, movement, hygiene or exercise.
Upper Arms
Skin may hang between the armpit and elbow even after significant fat loss.
Thighs
Loose inner-thigh skin can rub during walking or exercise.
Breasts
Weight loss can reduce breast volume while stretched skin remains. Some patients notice deflation, sagging or loss of upper-breast fullness.
Waist and Lower Back
Excess skin may extend around the hips, waist or buttocks, making a more extensive lower-body lift relevant.
Face and Neck
Loss of facial fat may reveal facial laxity or create a hollow appearance. This is sometimes described online as "Ozempic face," although facial changes can follow significant weight loss regardless of how the weight was lost.
Importantly, body contouring is not simply another form of weight-loss treatment.
Its purpose is generally to address skin and tissue that remain after weight reduction has already occurred.

What Body Contouring Procedures Can Be Considered After GLP-1 Weight Loss?
There is no single "GLP-1 body contouring surgery."
Treatment depends on where the excess skin is located and how extensive it is.
Tummy Tuck
An abdominoplasty removes excess abdominal skin and may address abdominal wall laxity when appropriate.
It can be relevant when weight loss leaves a substantial fold or loose skin across the lower abdomen.
Panniculectomy
A panniculectomy removes a hanging apron of abdominal skin known as a pannus.
Its goal may be more functional than cosmetic when excess tissue contributes to problems such as irritation or difficulty with hygiene.
It is not identical to a full tummy tuck.
Arm Lift
Brachioplasty removes excess skin from the upper arm.
This can be relevant when substantial weight loss leaves hanging tissue between the elbow and underarm.
Thigh Lift
A thigh lift removes and reshapes excess skin around the inner and/or outer thighs.
Breast Lift
Mastopexy raises and reshapes breasts affected by volume loss and skin laxity.
Some patients may discuss a lift alone, while others may be evaluated for augmentation, fat grafting or reduction depending on anatomy and goals.
ASPS notes that breast volume loss and sagging are increasingly being discussed by patients after GLP-1-associated weight reduction.
Lower Body Lift
A lower-body lift can address several connected areas, such as:
Abdomen
Waist
Hips
Outer thighs
Buttocks
It may involve a circumferential incision around part or all of the torso.
Upper Body Lift
This can address excess tissue across areas such as the upper back, lateral chest and surrounding regions.
Facial or Neck Procedures
Major weight reduction can also affect the face and neck.
Depending on anatomy, patients may ask about:
Facelift
Neck lift
Facial fat grafting
Eyelid surgery
Nonsurgical volume restoration
A responsible consultation should determine whether these changes are best addressed surgically, nonsurgically or not treated at all.

Why Post-GLP-1 Surgery Can Be More Complex Than Standard Cosmetic Surgery
Someone seeking a routine cosmetic tummy tuck and someone who has lost 80 or 100 pounds may have very different surgical needs.
Major weight loss can leave excess skin across several connected regions.
ASPS notes that post-weight-loss contouring may require extensive incisions, and procedures are frequently performed in stages rather than all at once.
That is important for medical tourists.
A patient may initially think:
"I want my stomach, arms, thighs and breasts fixed in one trip."
But combining too many procedures can lengthen anesthesia time, increase physiological stress and make recovery more difficult.
A qualified surgeon may recommend:
Stage 1: abdomen/lower body
Stage 2: breasts/upper body
Stage 3: arms or thighs
The exact strategy should be based on the individual's health and anatomy rather than travel convenience.

Do You Need to Reach Your Goal Weight Before Surgery?
Weight stability is an important part of post-weight-loss surgery planning.
ASPS identifies adults with stabilized weight as better candidates for body contouring.
If substantial additional weight loss occurs after surgery, more laxity can develop.
If significant weight is regained, the result can also change.
The surgeon may therefore ask about:
Current weight
Highest previous weight
Amount lost
How long the current weight has been stable
Whether the patient is still losing weight
GLP-1 dosage
Future treatment plan
Nutritional intake
Exercise habits
There is no universal timeline that applies to every GLP-1 patient.
The treating surgeon, prescribing clinician and anesthesia team should determine appropriate timing individually.

What Happens to GLP-1 Medication Before Surgery?
This question should be handled by the surgical and anesthesia teams rather than by following generic internet advice.
GLP-1 and related medications can affect gastrointestinal function, and perioperative management recommendations have evolved as clinicians have gained more experience with these drugs.
Patients should tell their surgeon and anesthesiologist:
Exactly which medication they take
Dose
Dosing schedule
Why it was prescribed
Whether the dose recently changed
Whether they experience nausea, vomiting, reflux, bloating or delayed stomach emptying
Whether they have diabetes
Do not independently stop prescription medication because a clinic coordinator, online forum or social-media post tells you to do so.
The appropriate perioperative plan should come from the clinicians responsible for your care.

Nutrition Matters More Than Many Patients Expect
Large weight loss can sometimes be associated with nutritional deficiencies, regardless of whether weight was lost through medication, bariatric surgery or another method.
That matters because wound healing requires adequate nutrition.
In 2026, ASPS highlighted recommendations emphasizing nutritional screening and personalized dietary planning for patients undergoing body contouring after significant weight loss. Protein, vitamins and minerals can all become relevant to healing.
Preoperative assessment may therefore include:
Nutritional history
Protein intake
Blood count
Iron status
Vitamin levels when clinically appropriate
Metabolic testing
Evaluation of previous bariatric surgery if applicable
Patients should be cautious of any clinic willing to schedule extensive surgery without obtaining an adequate medical history.

Are GLP-1 Users at Different Surgical Risk?
Research is still developing.
A 2026 study highlighted by ASPS examined patients undergoing panniculectomy following massive weight loss and found that GLP-1 use may be associated with differences in some postoperative complications, although the findings did not suggest a simple across-the-board increase in overall surgical risk.
The practical message is not that GLP-1 users should avoid surgery.
It is that surgeons need to know about medication use and incorporate it into surgical planning.
The evidence is evolving, and individualized medical assessment is more useful than broad assumptions such as "GLP-1 patients are safe" or "GLP-1 patients are high risk."

Frequently Asked Questions
Does Ozempic directly cause loose skin?
Loose skin is more accurately associated with substantial or rapid weight loss rather than being a unique direct effect of one medication. Skin may not contract fully after body volume decreases.

Is body contouring the same as liposuction?
No. Liposuction removes localized fat. Body-contouring surgery after major weight loss often involves removing significant amounts of excess skin. Some procedures may incorporate liposuction, but it cannot substitute for skin removal when substantial laxity is present.

What is the most common surgery after GLP-1 weight loss?
There is no single procedure appropriate for everyone. However, ASPS reported that tummy tuck was the body procedure most frequently discussed in GLP-1-related consultations among surveyed member surgeons, followed closely by breast surgery, arm lift and thigh lift.

Can several procedures be done at once?
Sometimes, but more is not necessarily better. The safe number and combination of procedures depend on medical history, surgical time, extent of tissue removal and other factors. Extensive post-weight-loss body contouring is often staged.

Should I stop Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro or Zepbound before surgery?
Do not make this decision yourself. Medication management should be coordinated between your prescribing clinician, surgeon and anesthesiologist.

How much does body contouring cost in Mexico?
Costs vary significantly by procedure. Current PlacidWay clinic data shows tummy-tuck quotes in Mexico from approximately $3,950 to $12,900, while larger multi-procedure plans can cost considerably more.

Is Mexico safe for plastic surgery?
Mexico has qualified plastic surgeons and established private hospitals, but quality varies by provider and facility. Safety should be evaluated at the surgeon and facility level rather than assuming that every clinic within a country meets the same standards.

Is Mexico better than Turkey for body contouring?
For many US and Canadian patients, Mexico may be more practical because of travel distance and follow-up access. For UK or European patients, Turkey may offer the geographic advantage. Surgeon expertise and aftercare should ultimately be more important than destination popularity.

How soon after reaching my goal weight should I consider surgery?
Weight should generally be stable before body contouring, but there is no single timeline appropriate to every patient. A plastic surgeon should evaluate your weight trajectory, medical health, medication use and nutritional status.
